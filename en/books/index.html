A History of Chinese Alcohol (Wang Saishi): From Prehistoric Remains to the Late Qing

Wang Saishi’s A History of Chinese Alcohol, published by Shandong University Press in 2010, is a 430,000-character survey from prehistoric remains to the late Qing and is presented as the first systematic history of alcohol in China. This article covers its editions, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.

Beishan Jiujing (Classic of Wine)

A three-volume brewing treatise by Zhu Gong of the Northern Song, also known as Beishan Jiujing; the upper volume discusses wine and brewing principles, the middle records qu-making, and the lower sets out the brewing process, making it a key text for understanding Song-era yellow wine.

On this page · 6Read BooksReviewedEN

Bencao Gangmu (wine sections)

Volume 25 of Li Shizhen’s Ming pharmacopoeia, in the fermented-and-brewed class of the grain section, collects entries on wine, shaojiu and grape wine with methods, properties and medicinal claims. It is a key text for Ming wine knowledge and for the “shaojiu began in the Yuan” account, but its pharmacology is not modern medicine.

Dongjing Menghua Lu: A Written Map of Bianjing, with Wine Houses as Coordinates

Meng Yuanlao’s Dongjing Menghua Lu, completed in 1147, is a ten-juan memoir of Northern Song Bianjing. Wine houses, licensed breweries, petty wine shops, night markets and festival drinking customs are among its most important material clues. This article covers editions, author, wine content, historical value and common misreadings.

Dream of the Red Chamber

Cao Xueqin's novel, written in the mid-18th century and printed in a 120-chapter edition in 1791; wine runs through banquets, festivals and everyday hospitality, with yellow wine, Huiquan wine, shaojiu and acacia-flower infusion each belonging to a different occasion. Read the wine passages together with character, ritual and edition.

David Wondrich’s Imbibe!, first published in 2007 and revised in 2015, uses Jerry Thomas’s 1862 How to Mix Drinks to reconstruct nineteenth-century American bar culture and the making of the classic cocktail. This article covers its editions, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.

Jin Ping Mei: Wine Tables, Trade Routes and Ming Life in a Novel of Manners

Jin Ping Mei, a Ming novel attributed to the pen name Lanling Xiaoxiaosheng, uses the rise and fall of Ximen Qing’s household to portray commerce, family, desire and urban life. Wine is both daily banquet fare and a tool of transaction, seduction and power. This article reads the novel through alcohol and food history, with editions, evidence limits and common misreadings.

A book that starts with plants and ends with drinks: wheat, corn, rice, sugarcane, agave, grapes, juniper and spices become spirits and wines through fermentation, distillation or infusion. It combines botany, craft and food history, and works best as an index and an introduction rather than a commercial distilling manual.

Wine and War

A narrative history of how French winegrowers, merchants and estates tried to preserve their vineyards, stocks and trade during the Nazi occupation of 1940–1944. Wine appears here as both a commodity and a symbol of national identity, and therefore as an object contested by occupiers, the Vichy regime, resisters and ordinary families.
