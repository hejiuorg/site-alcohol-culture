Nian Nu Jiao: Remembering the Red Cliff, Su Shi Pours Wine to the River Moon

Nian Nu Jiao was written in 1082, while Su Shi was exiled to Huangzhou. The song moves from the great river flowing east to Zhou Yu of the Three Kingdoms, then back to the poet with white hair, and ends with “I pour a cup of wine as a libation to the river moon”. Wine appears only at the end, not as a deep drink but as a cup poured out to the river and the moon.

This song is often treated as the model of the heroic ci: the great river flowing east, waves striking the shore, Zhou Yu destroying an enemy fleet amid laughter, a vast scale. But its ending is not heroic at all; it is low: with so much feeling, I should be laughed at, my hair turning white early. Life is like a dream; I pour a cup of wine as a libation to the river moon. Wine here is neither a victory toast nor a way to drown sorrow; it is a cup poured out to the river and the moon.

The song and the Red Cliff

Nian Nu Jiao is the name of the tune, and Remembering the Red Cliff is the title. The song was written in 1082, when Su Shi had already been exiled to Huangzhou for several years after the Wutai poetry case; he was forty-five. He visited the Red Nose Cliff outside Huangzhou, thought of the battle at the Red Cliff, and wrote this song.

It must be said that the Huangzhou Red Cliff Su Shi visited is not the battlefield of the Three Kingdoms. The historical Red Cliff is generally placed in what is now the city of Chibi in Hubei, while the Huangzhou Red Cliff is a riverside crag that took the name because it looked the part. The line people say, is the Red Cliff of Zhou Yu of the Three Kingdoms already leaves room: this is what others say, not a conclusion he verified. Later readers call the Huangzhou site the literary Red Cliff and the battlefield the military Red Cliff precisely to keep them apart.

Text

The great river flows east; its waves wash away all the gallant figures of a thousand ages. West of the old rampart, people say, is the Red Cliff of Zhou Yu of the Three Kingdoms. Broken rocks pierce the sky; startled waves strike the shore, rolling up a thousand piles of snow. Rivers and mountains are like a painting; in one moment, how many heroes! I think back to Gongjin in those years, when Xiao Qiao had just married him, handsome and full of spirit. With a feather fan and a black silk headcloth, amid talk and laughter, the masts and oars were reduced to flying ash. My spirit travels through the old kingdom; with so much feeling, I should be laughed at, my hair turning white early. Life is like a dream; I pour a cup of wine as a libation to the river moon.

This is the received text. Broken rocks pierce the sky also appears as broken rocks split the clouds, masts and oars also appears as strong crossbows, and life is like a dream also appears as the human world is like a dream. These variants change a local picture but not the overall structure.

The upper half: rivers, mountains and the old rampart

The opening two lines begin with the great river. “The great river flows east” is space; “its waves wash away all the gallant figures of a thousand ages” is time. What the water carries away is not only sand and stone but generations of people. The line places nature and history in one channel, and its force comes from scale, not adjectives.

“West of the old rampart, people say, is the Red Cliff of Zhou Yu of the Three Kingdoms” draws the view in to an old fortification. The old rampart is a former military work, west is a direction; the three words people say matter, because they show that the Red Cliff of Zhou Yu is an identification made by hearsay, not a settled geographical proof. Su Shi does not pretend to stand at the exact centre of the old battlefield; he writes what people say this place is.

“Broken rocks pierce the sky; startled waves strike the shore, rolling up a thousand piles of snow” writes the riverbank. The broken rocks rise as if piercing the sky; the startled waves strike the cliff and roll up layer upon layer of white foam like a thousand piles of snow. The three lines mix sight and sound: the rock is hard, the wave moves, the snow is white. They make the Red Cliff a place of powerful terrain, not merely a stretch of history.

“Rivers and mountains are like a painting; in one moment, how many heroes!” closes the upper half. “Rivers and mountains are like a painting” is both praise and a transition: however good the scenery, people are the subjects of history; “in one moment, how many heroes” brings forward the whole group of Three Kingdoms talents and prepares for Zhou Yu in the lower half.

The lower half: Zhou Yu and the self

I think back to Gongjin in those years turns time back to the Three Kingdoms. Gongjin is Zhou Yu's courtesy name. When Xiao Qiao had just married him, handsome and full of spirit writes Zhou Yu's youth and brilliance. There is a common misunderstanding here: Xiao Qiao did not marry Zhou Yu in the year of the Red Cliff battle; the just married in the song is a literary compression that packs Zhou Yu's youth and achievement into one picture, and need not be checked against a chronological table.

“With a feather fan and a black silk headcloth, amid talk and laughter, the masts and oars were reduced to flying ash” writes Zhou Yu's composure. The feather fan is a fan of feathers, the black silk headcloth a scholar's head covering; the outfit is that of a cultured commander, not a warrior in armour. Amid talk and laughter turns a great battle into easy conversation, and the enemy's warships are reduced to ash while people talk and laugh. This is literary exaggeration, and also Su Shi's imagining of Zhou Yu's bearing.

My spirit travels through the old kingdom; with so much feeling, I should be laughed at, my hair turning white early suddenly returns to himself. The old kingdom means the old territory of the Three Kingdoms, and travelling in spirit means a journey in the mind; with so much feeling, I should be laughed at is inverted, meaning people should laugh at me for being so full of feeling. Zhou Yu was young and successful, while he has already grown white hair; the more brilliant the history, the harder the present.

“Life is like a dream; I pour a cup of wine as a libation to the river moon” closes the song. “Life is like a dream” is a sigh, not a conclusion; a cup is a wine vessel, and to pour a libation is to spill wine on the ground or water as an offering. Su Shi does not drink the wine; he gives it to the river and the moon. This cup is neither a victory toast nor a way to drown sorrow, but an offering to history, to heroes and to himself.

What wine does at the end

The whole song mentions wine only at the end, and not as drinking but as a libation. The action moves wine from the mouth to the ground or the water: wine does not enter the body, it is given to the scenery and to time.

First, it turns personal feeling into a rite. What came before was remembering the past; the ending is an offering. Wine makes life is like a dream not just a sigh but an action that can be performed.

Second, it draws the heroic back into restraint. The first half has great scale, but the ending has no smashed cup, no deep drink, no long howl; a cup is lightly poured out, and the feeling becomes heavier.

Third, it places the person and history in the same position. Zhou Yu is gone, and Su Shi will be gone; the river and the moon remain. The wine is for them and for himself.

For another kind of moonlit drinking, read Li Bai's Drinking Alone under the Moon: that poem pulls the moon and the shadow in as companions, while this song offers wine to the river moon, a quieter gesture. For another Mid-Autumn song by Su Shi, see Shui Diao Ge Tou: that one ends with a wish that we may live long, this one with life is like a dream, and the temperature of the wine differs.

Variants and place

On variants, broken rocks pierce the sky also appears as broken rocks split the clouds; the first writes rocks rising high, the second writes rocks splitting like clouds. Masts and oars also appears as strong crossbows; the first means masts and oars, the second strong bows, and either can explain reduced to flying ash. Life is like a dream also appears as the human world is like a dream, a difference in outlook.

On place, the Huangzhou Red Cliff and the Three Kingdoms Red Cliff are not the same site. Treating this song as written standing on the battlefield site ignores the caution of people say in the text, and misses Su Shi's actual situation: he was on a riverbank near his place of exile, borrowing a place with the same name to write his own historical imagination.

Common misreadings

First, taking the Huangzhou Red Cliff as the battlefield of the Three Kingdoms. They are not the same place.

Second, taking Xiao Qiao had just married him as a marriage in the year of the battle. It is literary compression, not chronology.

Third, taking the feather fan and black headcloth as Zhuge Liang's image alone. In this song it describes Zhou Yu.

Fourth, taking the libation to the river moon as drinking. A libation is wine poured out, not swallowed.

Fifth, reading a heroic ci as free of sorrow. The heroism always carries the disappointment of white hair grown early.

For Nian Nu Jiao, begin with the poet and date. Then look at the text and translation, and finally ask what it is cited for here. The answers point to different choices, not to a single score.
