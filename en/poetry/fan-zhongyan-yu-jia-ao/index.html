Yu Jia Ao: Fan Zhongyan and a Cup of Cloudy Wine at the Frontier

Yu Jia Ao describes autumn at the north-western frontier: geese leave without lingering, border sounds rise, the lone city closes. The lower half sets “a cup of cloudy wine, home ten thousand miles away” against “Mount Yanran not yet inscribed, no way to return”, and ends on “the general's white hair, the soldiers' tears”. A single cup measures the distance between home and the frontier.

This song writes the autumn of a frontier garrison, but its centre is not scenery; it is a cup of wine. “A cup of cloudy wine, home ten thousand miles away” has only a few words, yet it puts a cup and a home ten thousand miles away in one line: the cup is small, the distance vast; the wine can enter the mouth, the home cannot be returned to. Fan Zhongyan served on the north-western frontier, and the frontier city, horns and white hair in this song are not inventions.

The song and the frontier

Yu Jia Ao is the tune name, and Autumn Thoughts is the title. Fan Zhongyan (989–1052) was in charge of north-western defence during the Kangding and Qingli years of Emperor Renzong's reign, resisting the Western Xia; this experience gave him direct knowledge of frontier life. The song is generally dated to his frontier service, though the exact year is disputed.

Frontier songs are not many in the Song, and this one is often treated as an early masterpiece of the kind. It writes no great battle and no victory, only an autumn night in a frontier city: geese flying south, horns sounding, gates closed, one cup of wine, frost on the ground.

Text

Autumn at the frontier looks different; the Hengyang geese go south without lingering. Border sounds rise on every side with the horn; among a thousand peaks, long smoke, setting sun, the lone city closes. A cup of cloudy wine, home ten thousand miles away; Mount Yanran not yet inscribed, no way to return. The Qiang flute drifts, frost fills the ground; no one sleeps; the general's white hair, the soldiers' tears.

This is the received text. The frontier means the north-western border; the Hengyang geese go refers to geese flying south, traditionally said to stop at the return-south peak at Hengyang; border sounds are the wind, horns and neighing particular to the frontier; a thousand peaks are layered mountains; the lone city closes means the gates are shut; Mount Yanran not yet inscribed uses the story of Dou Xian, who defeated the Xiongnu and had his achievement carved on Mount Yanran, meaning that the work is not yet done; the Qiang flute is a frontier flute.

The upper half: frontier scenery

“Autumn at the frontier looks different” opens with a summary. Different is the key word: autumn at the frontier is not like autumn in the central plains, and the difference is not only scenery but life. The word takes the reader from a familiar season to an unfamiliar place.

“The Hengyang geese go south without lingering” writes the geese. They fly south and leave without any attachment to this place; without lingering is both the geese's attitude and the human feeling: even the geese will not stay, yet people must.

“Border sounds rise on every side with the horn; among a thousand peaks, long smoke, setting sun, the lone city closes” writes sound and space. Border sounds on every side join the horn; among layered peaks, long smoke rises, the sun sets, and the lone city shuts its gates. These lines have hearing, sight and action, and make the frontier city an enclosed world: once the gates close, outside are the open country and the enemy, inside the garrison and the long night.

The lower half: a cup of cloudy wine

“A cup of cloudy wine, home ten thousand miles away” is the most famous line. Cloudy wine is unfiltered wine, not a famous bottle; a cup is a small quantity; home ten thousand miles away is a great distance. The three phrases together make a sharp contrast: only one cup of wine, but home is ten thousand miles off.

The line does not say I miss home; it measures by quantity and distance. Wine can be finished in one swallow, but homesickness cannot be solved by one cup. Wine here is not a tool for dispelling sorrow but a ruler for measuring distance.

“Mount Yanran not yet inscribed, no way to return” gives the reason he cannot go home. Mount Yanran not yet inscribed uses the story of Dou Xian: the achievement is not complete and the inscription has not been carved; no way to return means there is no way back. No way does not mean he does not want to return; it means he cannot. Homesickness is therefore not only a feeling but a situation.

Mount Yanran not yet inscribed

The phrase joins personal homesickness to the work of the state. Dou Xian of the Eastern Han defeated the Xiongnu, climbed Mount Yanran and carved a record of his achievement; later ages use inscribing Mount Yanran for winning frontier merit. Fan Zhongyan uses it to say that he has not yet completed his frontier duty, so he cannot speak of returning home.

This does not mean he does not miss home. On the contrary, it is precisely because he misses home that he needs a higher reason to hold it down. The song neither glorifies war nor belittles homesickness; it puts both in one person and lets the reader see the conflict.

The general and the soldiers

“The Qiang flute drifts, frost fills the ground” writes sound and ground. The Qiang flute sounds long and far, frost has covered the ground; time moves from dusk to deep night and the temperature falls. No one sleeps states insomnia directly: neither the general nor the soldiers are asleep.

The general's white hair, the soldiers' tears puts two kinds of people in one line. The general's hair has turned white from worry, the soldiers weep from homesickness; one is above, one below, but in the same frontier city neither can sleep. The ending offers no solution; it simply stops on white hair and tears.

Common misreadings

First, taking cloudy wine as poor-quality wine. Cloudy means unfiltered, not spoiled; at the frontier it is already a rare thing.

Second, taking Mount Yanran not yet inscribed as bellicosity. It writes an unfinished duty, not a desire for war.

Third, taking the general's white hair and the soldiers' tears as a contrast between two opposed kinds of people. They share one predicament and express it differently.

Fourth, taking the song as pure landscape. Behind the scenery are the frontier situation and homesickness.

Fifth, taking the Hengyang geese as having really reached Hengyang. It is a common classical way of saying geese fly south and need not be checked geographically.
