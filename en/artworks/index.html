An Interior with a Woman Drinking and Two Men: A Glass of Wine in Delft

Pieter de Hooch's interior, painted around 1658 and now in the National Gallery, London, shows a woman with her back to us holding a glass while two men use pipes as instruments. The picture stays open between singing, drinking and suggestion, and infrared imaging reveals a figure the painter removed.

Brouwer's The Drinker: A Tavern and a Face

A tavern genre scene by the Flemish painter Adriaen Brouwer, represented here by the Mauritshuis's Drunken Peasants at an Inn and often discussed under titles such as The Drinker. Drink, tobacco and noise strip everyone of sobriety; the picture is both comic observation and a cool look at human weakness.

Caravaggio's Bacchus: A Glass of Wine and a Basket of Fruit

Caravaggio's Bacchus, painted around 1598 and now in the Uffizi Galleries in Florence, shows a young wine god offering a glass to the viewer beside a minutely painted basket of fruit. It reads both as an invitation and as a puzzle about drunkenness and classical models.

Heda's Still Life with Drinking Vessels: A Roemer and a Watch

A still life by the Dutch painter Willem Claesz. Heda, represented here by the Mauritshuis's Still Life with a Roemer and Watch. A large drinking glass, silver, food and a watch are painted in a near-monochrome light; the picture is both a study of drinking vessels and a still-life tradition about time and moderation.

L'Absinthe: Degas Paints Two People Sitting Together and Looking Past Each Other

Edgar Degas's canvas of 1875–1876, originally titled In a Café, hangs in the Musée d'Orsay. A actress and a painter share a Paris café table with a glass of absinthe; the subject is not the drink but the solitude of the modern city.

Li Bai Drunk: a subject painted again and again

Starting from Su Liupeng's Li Bai Drunk, a hanging scroll of 1844 in the Shanghai Museum (ink and colour on paper, 204.8 by 93.9 cm), the page traces the subject across centuries and distinguishes it from Liang Kai's Li Bai Walking and Reciting in Tokyo and a Boston scroll of Li Bai drinking with Emperor Minghuang.

Literary Gathering: Emperor Huizong Paints a Scholars' Dinner as an Ideal of Civil Order

Traditionally attributed to Song emperor Huizong, this ink-and-colour silk scroll in Taipei shows a garden gathering of scholars. A large table holds wine cups, tea ware and fruit; servants attend. It is less a record of one dinner than an imperial image of cultured governance.

The Absinthe Drinker: Manet Puts a Marginal Man on a History-Painting Scale

Édouard Manet's early canvas of about 1859, now at the Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek, shows a single cloaked man, an empty bottle at his feet and a small glass on a ledge. Rejected by the 1859 Salon, it became a founding image of modern-life painting.

The Eighteen Scholars: A Tang Political Portrait Turned into Four Scenes of Literati Life

A set of four hanging scrolls in Taipei, formerly labelled Song but now often dated to the Ming, depicts the Eighteen Scholars of Tang Taizong in garden gatherings organised around the qin, chess, calligraphy and painting. The subject began as political propaganda and became a template for literati life.

The Night Café: Van Gogh Paints a Gaslit Room as an All-Night Fever

Vincent van Gogh's September 1888 canvas, now at the Yale University Art Gallery, shows the interior of an all-night café in Arles: red walls, a green billiard table, four lemon-yellow gas lamps and exhausted patrons. The artist said he wanted to paint "the terrible passions of humanity."

The Night Revels of Han Xizai: a banquet turned into a painting

A silk handscroll attributed to Gu Hongzhong of the Southern Tang, 28.7 by 335.5 cm, in the Palace Museum, Beijing, graded first class A. The page reads the five scenes of listening, dancing, resting, piping and farewell, explains how wine warmers and cups pace the night, and reviews the Song-copy and surveillance debates.

The Peasant Wedding: Bruegel Paints a Village Feast as a Cross-Section of Society

Pieter Bruegel the Elder's 1568 panel in Vienna shows a Flemish peasant wedding. The bride sits before a green cloth; the bridegroom is absent by custom; two men carry porridge on a door taken off its hinges while a servant pours beer. The snapshot look is carefully composed.

The Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove and Rong Qiqi: a brick relief in a Southern Dynasties tomb

A moulded brick relief excavated in 1960 from a Southern Dynasties tomb at Xishanqiao, Nanjing. The Nanjing Museum's two groups measure 78 by 242.5 cm and 78 by 241.5 cm, with name labels beside the figures. The page reads the eight poses, the place of wine among them, and the disputes over tomb date, patron and lost model.

The Wedding at Cana: A Wall-Sized Feast with a Miracle at the Edge

Paolo Veronese's 1562–1563 canvas for the refectory of San Giorgio Maggiore, now at the Louvre, turns the Gospel miracle of water into wine into a Venetian banquet. Christ sits at the centre, but the wine moves through servants, cupbearers and guests.

Velázquez's The Triumph of Bacchus: Bringing Myth into the Crowd

Velázquez's painting of about 1628–1629, now in the Museo del Prado, places Bacchus among a group of Spanish peasants. Cups, crowning and drinking bring myth down to everyday life; the picture is at once a parody of the classical figure and a new telling of Bacchic culture.

Vermeer's The Glass of Wine: A Drink and a Window

Vermeer's interior, painted around 1658–1660 and now in the Gemäldegalerie in Berlin, shows a woman at a table holding a glass while a man pours from a jug. The picture places drinking, hospitality and Dutch Golden Age domestic life in the same window light.
