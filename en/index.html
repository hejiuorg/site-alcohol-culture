Reading a city through its bars

A bar is a slice of city life: its opening hours, regulars, drinks list and seating together reveal who a neighbourhood is made of, when it comes alive and how it treats guests. This article compares the pub, the café, the bar and the beer hall, and suggests how to observe them and how to read their history.

Time in a cup

From the spring wine of the Book of Songs to the golden goblets of Tang poetry, the ancients wrote seasons, farm work and gatherings into their drinking. This article discusses only what the documents can support, separating text, edition and present-day imagination.

A history of taverns: from wine shops and inns to bars

The form of a tavern is shaped by cities, transport, taxation and regulation. Comparing Chinese wine houses, European inns and taverns, 19th-century bars and American saloons, this article explains why the same function took different shapes, and how to read an old tavern.

Why does a drink taste of a place?

Place enters a drink through climate, soil, variety, microbes and craft; terroir is a clue to understanding origin, not a guarantee of quality. This article takes wine, whisky, baijiu and yellow wine to unpack the layers behind “a taste of place”.
