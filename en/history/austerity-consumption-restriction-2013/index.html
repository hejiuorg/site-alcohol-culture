The 2013 restrictions on official consumption and the baijiu adjustment

After the Central Committee's eight-point rules of late 2012, official spending and receptions were tightened in 2013, and official banquets and premium baijiu consumption contracted sharply. This essay explains the policy background, the industry response and the structural changes of the adjustment period.

On 4 December 2012 the Political Bureau of the 18th Central Committee approved the eight-point rules on improving work style and maintaining close ties with the masses; the document was issued on 11 December. In 2013 a series of detailed rules on official spending, official receptions and government buildings followed, and official banquets and the official use of alcohol contracted quickly. Premium baijiu, long dependent on government and business occasions, was hit first, and the industry moved from its "golden decade" into an adjustment period.

Background

After 2003 the baijiu industry expanded rapidly in what became known as the golden decade. Premium products raised prices year after year, and official banquets, business entertainment and gift demand formed an important part of the market. Distributors held stock in the expectation of further price rises, group-buying channels flourished, and informal approval channels coexisted with formal retail. That demand structure made the industry unusually sensitive to policy change. The baijiu plasticiser incident of November 2012 also damaged consumer trust, so policy pressure and a trust crisis arrived together in 2013. The plasticiser incident is therefore an essential part of the background.

Course and timeline

  • 4 December 2012: the Political Bureau approves the eight-point rules; they are issued on 11 December, calling for simpler travel, fewer meetings and tighter reception standards.
  • April 2013: the Ministry of Finance discloses a central-level budget of 7.969 billion yuan for official overseas travel, vehicles and receptions, including 1.434 billion yuan for official receptions, 126 million yuan less than the previous year's outturn.
  • November 2013: the CPC Central Committee and the State Council issue the Regulations on Practising Economy and Opposing Waste in Party and State Bodies, covering receptions, meetings and travel.
  • 8 December 2013: the General Offices of the CPC Central Committee and the State Council issue the Provisions on Domestic Official Receptions. Article 10 states that working meals must not provide cigarettes or high-end alcohol and must not use private clubs or high-spending restaurants.
  • Late 2013 to 2014: wholesale prices of premium baijiu fall, distributors destock, and producers shift toward mass-market prices, e-commerce and banquet occasions.
  • 2015: output among large baijiu enterprises recovers, and the industry enters a weak recovery marked by sharper divergence.

Key people and institutions

On the policy side the key bodies were the Political Bureau, the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and the National Supervisory Commission, the Ministry of Finance and the National Government Offices Administration. On the industry side they were the China Alcoholic Drinks Association, the China Alcoholic Drinks Circulation Association and leading producers such as Moutai and Wuliangye. In 2013 Moutai's chairman, Yuan Renguo, still stressed price discipline, but market prices had already broken away from the company's guidance. Wuliangye faced weaker sales of premium products. Distributors, group-buying agents and restaurants were the end of the transmission chain and carried the inventory burden most directly.

Concrete changes for the industry

First, output and revenue kept growing, but growth collapsed. According to the China Alcoholic Drinks Circulation Association, large baijiu enterprises produced 1226.2 ten-thousand kilolitres (12.262 million kilolitres) in 2013, with sales of 501.8 billion yuan and profit of 80.487 billion yuan; growth in every indicator fell sharply. Second, company results diverged. Guizhou Moutai's 2013 revenue was 30.922 billion yuan, up 16.88%, while Wuliangye's revenue was 24.717 billion yuan, down 9.13%, and its net profit was 7.973 billion yuan, down about one fifth. Third, the price system was rebuilt. The market price of 53-degree Feitian Moutai fell from a 2012 peak of about 2200 yuan to a range of 850 to 1200 yuan by late 2013. Fourth, catering changed. National catering revenue in 2013 was 2,539.2 billion yuan, up 9.0%, but revenue at catering enterprises above the designated size was 818.1 billion yuan, down 1.8%. Fifth, channels and products moved downmarket. Bare-bottle, small-format and everyday baijiu gained shelf space, and consumers became more conscious of choosing by occasion, as discussed in choosing your first baijiu.

Disputes and later assessments

One dispute concerns the share of official consumption. Figures of 30% to 40% are often quoted, but definitions differ and most are estimates. A second dispute concerns causation: attributing the whole adjustment to the restrictions ignores overcapacity, channel inventory and the plasticiser incident. A third concerns evaluation. One view holds that the policy punctured a bubble and forced producers to face real consumers; another holds that it caused price inversion, distributor losses and pressure on local tax revenue in the short run. After 2015, Moutai, Wuliangye and other leaders recovered through volume control, price increases and channel reform, but concentration rose and smaller distilleries did not escape pressure.

Common misreadings

First, the eight-point rules are not a prohibition law. They regulate work style and official receptions; they do not ban citizens from drinking or ban the sale of alcohol. Second, "three public expenditures" are not all public spending, still less all baijiu consumption. Third, baijiu output did not fall in 2013; what fell was growth, prices and premium demand. Fourth, the shock did not hit premium brands alone: group buying, catering, packaging and gift chains were all affected. Fifth, explaining the adjustment solely as a policy result obscures the capacity and inventory problems that had already appeared.
