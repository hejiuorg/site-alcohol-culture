The 1989 fifth national wine evaluation: seventeen famous wines and the end of the system

The fifth national wine evaluation, held in Hefei, Anhui, in January 1989 and organised by the China Food Industry Association, selected 17 national famous wines, all baijiu; national evaluations were discontinued afterwards. This essay explains the list, the background and why the system ended.

In January 1989 the fifth national wine evaluation was held in Hefei, Anhui. It selected 17 national famous wines, all of them baijiu, later known as the seventeen famous wines. It was both the high point of the national evaluations and their end: after this meeting the state held no further national evaluation of the kind, and the famous-wine list was fixed at 1989.

Background

After 1984 baijiu output and prices rose quickly, and the commercial value of the famous-wine title reached a peak. Local plants expanded, advertising and packaging competition intensified, and many products imitating famous-wine names and designs appeared. The industry needed a new evaluation to reconfirm the quality hierarchy. At the same time the evaluation faced questions: whether samples represented products on sale, whether judges applied a common standard, and whether the title was over-marketed.

In 1988 famous-wine prices were gradually liberalised, and the market began to replace administrative recognition. In September 1988 the Ministry of Light Industry brought together the Ministry of Commerce, the State Bureau of Technical Supervision and the China Food Industry Association to study the rules for a new evaluation. The fifth meeting was therefore both a quality contest and the last concentrated operation of the planned-era system for certifying famous wines.

Course and timeline

From 10 to 19 January 1989 the fifth national wine evaluation took place in Hefei, organised by the China Food Industry Association, with Shen Yifang as head of the expert group. It was also described as the 1988 national quality baijiu evaluation. Judging continued to use coded blind tasting and aroma grouping; samples were classified by aroma type, saccharifying agent and alcohol strength, scored independently and then ranked.

The meeting selected 17 national famous wines, all baijiu: Moutai, Fenjiu, Wuliangye, Yanghe Daqu, Jiannanchun, Gujing Gongjiu, Dongjiu, Xifeng, Luzhou Laojiao Tequ, Quanxing Daqu, Shuanggou Daqu, special Huanghelou, Langjiu, Wuling, Baofeng, Songhe Liangye and Tuopai Qujiu. A number of national quality wines were also selected, commonly recorded as 53, though sources differ on the count. One participant recalled a margin of 1.2 points between a famous wine and a quality wine.

The timeline runs as follows: September 1988, rules studied; 10 to 19 January 1989, judging; late January, results announced; afterwards, no further national evaluation; the 1990s, famous-wine firms move into market competition; after the 2000s, the seventeen become a fixed concept in collecting and auction.

Key people and institutions

Shen Yifang headed the expert group at this meeting and had long worked on baijiu flavour and technology. Zhou Henggang had earlier led the technical work of the national evaluations and promoted examiner qualification, aroma grouping and coded samples. The China Food Industry Association hosted the meeting, while the Ministry of Light Industry, the Ministry of Commerce and the State Bureau of Technical Supervision took part in drafting the rules.

The institutional shift also matters. In the late 1980s government departments gradually withdrew from detailed prize-giving, and industry associations and technical experts took on more of the organisation. This raised professionalism but weakened the administrative authority of the evaluation. After the fifth meeting, famous wines were no longer certified by a single national event, and quality assessment moved towards standards, certification and market reputation.

Concrete changes for the industry

First, the famous-wine list became fixed. The seventeen became a standard concept in collecting, auction, local gazetteers and company history. Second, the evaluation left the stage. The state no longer certified famous wines through national meetings, and firms turned to advertising, distribution and brand building. Third, the price system changed. After famous-wine prices were liberalised in 1988, the gap between famous and ordinary wines widened, and the premium price band of the 1990s took shape. Fourth, the aroma map hardened. Strong aroma accounted for most of the seventeen, while sauce, light, Feng and Dong aromas each had representatives, and aroma became the main framework through which consumers understood baijiu. Fifth, local economies were affected. Firms on the list often became pillars of local finance and employment.

Disputes and later assessments

First, there is no single official explanation for why the evaluations stopped. Common explanations include cost, doubts about fairness, market reform and institutional restructuring. Second, the list of seventeen circulates in several versions, and whether individual products were included, and in what order, is disputed. Third, the number of quality wines differs between sources and has never been systematically compiled. Fourth, the 1.2-point margin is often quoted, but it only shows how close one group was. Fifth, treating the seventeen as a quality ranking is a misreading: it is the result of one selection, in one year, under one procedure.

Common misreadings

One: "The seventeen famous wines were seventeen new brands chosen in 1989." Most had already appeared in earlier meetings; this meeting kept some and added others. Two: "The fifth evaluation judged only baijiu." The scope still included yellow wine and wine; only the final national famous-wine list was entirely baijiu. Three: "China never judged wine again." The national evaluation stopped, but industry associations, local bodies and commercial competitions continued. Four: "Being on the list means the best quality today." The title is a historical honour, and ownership change, capacity expansion and process change all affect present quality. Five: "All seventeen are strong aroma." The list covers sauce, light, strong, Feng and Dong aromas; strong aroma is only the largest group.
