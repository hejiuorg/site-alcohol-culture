The Founding of the Qingdao Brewery (1903): German and British Merchants and China's First Continuously Operating Brewery

On 15 August 1903 German and British merchants founded the Germania Brewery Company, Qingdao, at what is now 56 Dengzhou Road, with initial annual output of about 2000 tonnes of pale and dark beer. In 1906 its beer won an award at the Munich exhibition; in 1945 the plant was renamed Qingdao Brewery and in 1949 became the state-owned Qingdao Brewery. It is the first Chinese brewery still operating today.

On 15 August 1903 German and British merchants founded the Germania Brewery Company, Qingdao, at what is now 56 Dengzhou Road in Qingdao. It is usually called China's first brewery still in continuous operation: not a short experimental brew but a modern factory with a stable product line and sales network.

Date and place

The founding date is 15 August 1903, and the place was Qingdao, then a German-leased territory. The company was set up through the Hong Kong Anglo-German Brewery Company, with German and British merchants providing the capital and German capital dominant; according to local media, the share capital raised was 400,000 Mexican silver dollars.

The site was on Millerstrasse, today 56 Dengzhou Road. The area was close to the Laoshan mineral water source and the port, convenient for water supply, transport and sales. Qingdao's German-style urban planning and the foreign consumer community in the leased territory also gave beer an early market.

Background: modern beer enters China

The early spread of beer in China was closely tied to treaty ports, leased territories and foreign garrisons. In the late nineteenth century, foreign or foreign-backed breweries and small-scale brewing appeared in Shanghai, Tianjin and Qingdao; beer was first a drink for foreign residents and only later entered Chinese urban consumption.

Qingdao's conditions were distinctive: the German leased territory brought German brewing tradition and equipment, Laoshan mineral water provided a stable supply, and the port made it easy to import malt and hops. Together these made Qingdao one of the most concentrated starting points of modern Chinese brewing.

Course: building the plant in 1903

The new company was named the Germania Brewery Company, Qingdao, and produced pale and dark beer. Local media record initial annual output of about 2000 tonnes; the company website stresses the use of Laoshan mineral water and German brewing technique. Early equipment, yeast and malt were largely imported, and technicians were mainly German.

In 1906 the company's beer won a gold award at the Munich world beer festival exhibition. The award mattered greatly to the early brand: it raised Qingdao beer from a local product of the leased territory to a commodity with international judging endorsement, and provided a basis for later exports and brand narrative.

Early production and brand

Early products were mainly pale lager, with dark beer as well. Lager brewing needs low-temperature fermentation and storage, which makes high demands on equipment and energy; Qingdao's German-style buildings, underground cellars and stable water supply met those conditions. Packaging moved gradually from wooden barrels to glass bottles, and sales extended from the leased territory to coastal cities and export markets.

'Germania' was the early brand name. In 1945 the plant was renamed Qingdao Brewery; after Qingdao was liberated on 2 June 1949 it became the state-owned Qingdao Brewery. The brand name 'Tsingtao Beer' continued, becoming a shared marker of the city and the product.

Influence: from leased-territory plant to national brand

First, it showed that China could produce beer continuously to modern factory standards. Earlier attempts were mostly short-lived or small-scale; the continuous operation of the Qingdao brewery turned beer from 'a foreigners' drink' into an industrial product that could be supplied steadily in Chinese cities.

Second, it bound Qingdao to beer. The old plant on Dengzhou Road, its early buildings and cellars later became industrial heritage; in 2006 the 'early buildings of the Qingdao Brewery' were listed in the sixth batch of national key cultural relic protection units.

Third, it left both a brand and an institutional legacy. On the brand side, Tsingtao Beer became one of the earliest Chinese beer brands with national and international recognition; on the institutional side, factory management, quality control and export experience provided a template for the later Chinese brewing industry.

Fourth, its historical narrative itself deserves study. The claim of 'China's first brewery' needs qualification: other foreign breweries existed in the late nineteenth century; Qingdao's distinctiveness lies in 'continuous operation to the present', not in being absolutely first.

Sources and disputes

First, 'first' needs qualification. Qingdao government heritage materials call it 'China's first brewery still in continuous operation'; the qualifier is 'still in continuous operation'. Saying only 'China's first brewery' would conflict with records of other early foreign breweries.

Second, the founding parties need separating. The company was set up through the Hong Kong Anglo-German Brewery Company, with German and British merchants providing capital and German capital dominant; describing it simply as 'founded by Germans' or 'founded by Britons' is inaccurate.

Third, early output and capital figures come from different sources. The 400,000 Mexican silver dollars and 2000 tonnes of annual output appear in local media reports, while the company website and government materials do not list every item; this essay cites them with their source and does not treat them as audited conclusions.

Fourth, the name and level of the 1906 award need care. Reports mostly say 'gold award at the Munich world beer festival exhibition', but the exact award category, exhibition name and judging records require the fair archives of the time to confirm.

Timeline

  • 1903: on 15 August the Germania Brewery Company, Qingdao, builds its plant on Dengzhou Road.
  • 1906: its beer wins a gold award at the Munich world beer festival exhibition.
  • 1945: the plant is renamed Qingdao Brewery.
  • 1949: Qingdao is liberated on 2 June and the plant becomes the state-owned Qingdao Brewery.
  • 2006: the 'early buildings of the Qingdao Brewery' are listed in the sixth batch of national key cultural relic protection units.
