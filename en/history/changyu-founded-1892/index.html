The Founding of Changyu (1892): The Start of Modern Chinese Winemaking

In 1892 the overseas Chinese businessman Zhang Bishi invested 3 million taels of silver to found the Changyu Winery at Zhifu Bay in Yantai, Shandong, widely regarded as the start of industrial winemaking in China. After three rounds of vine introduction and three attempts to hire a winemaker, the company built an underground cellar between 1894 and 1905, produced wine in 1899 and brandy in 1914, and won a grand prize at the 1915 Panama-Pacific exposition.

In 1892 the overseas Chinese businessman Zhang Bishi invested 3 million taels of silver to found the Changyu Winery at Zhifu Bay in Yantai, Shandong. It is widely regarded as the starting point of industrial winemaking in China: not the expansion of a traditional brewery but a new enterprise that introduced vines, built a cellar, hired a winemaker and organised production and sales on industrial lines.

Date and place

The founding year is 1892 and the place is Yantai, Shandong. Yantai's maritime climate, weathered granite soils and port conditions were seen by Zhang Bishi as a combination suited to wine grapes and export. In 1891, while in Yantai to discuss railway and mining matters, he took the opportunity to inspect local viticulture and soil hydrology, and decided the following year to invest in a winery.

Three dates need separating: 1892 is the founding year; 1899 is the company's first wine release; 1894 to 1905 is the construction period of the underground cellar. Treating 'founding' as 'production' distorts the timeline.

Background: late Qing industry and wine

In the late nineteenth century, under the Self-Strengthening and industrialisation movements, the Qing encouraged factory building, and new industrial sectors appeared in the treaty ports. Wine was one of them: it served foreigners in China and consumers beyond traditional huangjiu, and was seen as a substitute for imports that could retain economic rights.

China already had scattered winemaking attempts, mostly by missions, foreign firms or small workshops; Changyu's significance was to organise planting, vinification, ageing and sales into one company and to build a cellar and hire winemakers on industrial standards. This model differed from the traditional 'shop in front, workshop behind' and came closer to a vertically integrated modern enterprise.

Course: from inspection to founding

Zhang Bishi's founding process was highly personal. According to the Changyu Wine Culture Museum account, after inspecting Yantai in 1891 he concluded that the area had the natural conditions for wine grapes and winemaking; in 1892 he put up 3 million taels of silver and founded China's first grape winemaking company.

The first difficulty was raw material. In the early years Changyu introduced 124 varieties and 690,000 vines from Europe, but most died because they did not adapt; a second purchase from another source also failed; in the third year grafting onto local Yantai mountain grapes finally produced vines suited to the local environment. The three-introduction account comes from company records, and the exact variety list and survival rates need original archives to verify.

The second difficulty was people. Zhang Bishi hired foreign winemakers at high cost: the first, from England, died on the way from a tooth extraction; the second, from the Netherlands, worked for two years and was judged unsuitable; finally Baron Babo, an Austro-Hungarian consular official in Yantai from a winemaking family, became the first winemaker. This 'three attempts to hire a winemaker' story is retold with legendary detail, but it reflects the real shortage of modern winemaking talent in China at the time.

Building the plant: vines, winemaker, cellar

Changyu's early construction can be summed up as vines, winemaker and cellar.

Vines meant varieties and stock. Introduction, failure, reintroduction and finally grafting to local conditions formed an early experiment in modern Chinese wine-grape cultivation. Part of the variety lineage of today's Yantai region can be traced to this introduction and selection.

The winemaker meant technique. Baron Babo brought not only fermentation and ageing methods but cellar design, temperature and humidity control and hygiene standards, none of which had counterparts in traditional huangjiu workshops.

The cellar meant the underground great cellar. Company materials state that from 1894 to 1905, moving from hand digging to foreign methods and then to a Chinese-Western hybrid, it took eleven years to complete the first underground great cellar in Asia. The cellar was not only for storage: it determined the stability of temperature and humidity during ageing, and it was the dividing line between industrial and workshop production.

Influence: the starting point of industrial winemaking

First, it established the template of 'company, cellar and winemaker'. Later Chinese wine enterprises mostly followed the path of vine introduction, cellar construction, hiring winemakers and building brands.

Second, it put Yantai on the map of Chinese wine regions. Changyu's vineyards and cellars were concentrated in Yantai, making the port city the geographical origin of modern winemaking; today's wine tourism and regional narrative in Yantai still rest on this foundation.

Third, it changed the way 'firsts' were narrated. Wine in 1899, brandy in 1914 and a grand prize at the Panama-Pacific exposition in 1915 were arranged by the company into a continuous timeline and became a model of the 'national goods' narrative.

Fourth, it left industrial heritage that can be visited. In 1992 Changyu opened a wine culture museum in the old plant, turning company history, cellars and tasting into a public cultural space; the museum and the old plant together form the core of Yantai's winemaking heritage.

Sources and disputes

First, 'China's first wine enterprise' needs qualification. Changyu was the first modern company to organise winemaking industrially, but foreign firms, missions and small winemakers already existed in the late Qing; 'first' refers to an industrial enterprise, not to 'the first bottle of wine'.

Second, founding year and production year should not be mixed. The company dates its founding to 1892, but the first wine came in 1899; saying that wine was made in 1892, or that the company was founded in 1899, would both be wrong.

Third, the 3 million taels and the vine figures come from company narrative. The museum and company publish these numbers, but the original ledgers, vine lists and survival rates are not public; this page cites them with their source and does not treat them as independently audited conclusions.

Fourth, the category of the 1915 award needs precision. Changyu received the highest grade, the grand prize, at the Panama-Pacific exposition, not a generic 'gold medal'; details are on this site's 1915 Panama-Pacific exposition page.

Fifth, corporate narrative tends to absolutise 'firsts'. Chinese winemaking evolved through several companies, foreign firms and local workshops; Changyu's starting-point significance holds, but it should not erase contemporary attempts.

Timeline

  • 1891: Zhang Bishi inspects viticulture and soil hydrology in Yantai.
  • 1892: he invests 3 million taels and founds Changyu.
  • 1894: construction of the underground great cellar begins.
  • 1899: the company's first wine release.
  • 1905: the underground great cellar is completed.
  • 1914: brandy is produced.
  • 1915: a grand prize at the Panama-Pacific exposition.
  • 1992: the Changyu Wine Culture Museum opens in the old plant.
