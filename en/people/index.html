PeopleReviewedEN

André Simon

French-born British wine merchant, writer and bibliophile (1877–1970) who co-founded the Wine & Food Society in 1933, wrote more than a hundred books and pamphlets, and used trade archives and bibliography to turn wine history into a verifiable discipline.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

André Tchelistcheff

Russian-born American winemaker (1901–1994) who became chief winemaker at Beaulieu Vineyard in Napa Valley in 1938, brought cold fermentation, malolactic control and small French oak to California, and mentored generations of Napa winemakers.

On this page · 7Read PeopleReviewedEN

Angelo Gaja

Italian winemaker and estate owner (born 1940) who took over the family's Barbaresco estate in 1961, used low yields, French barriques, single vineyards and cross-regional investments to transform Piedmontese wine, and in 1996 declassified three single-vineyard wines from Barbaresco DOCG to Langhe DOC, sparking a continuing traditionalist-modernist debate.

On this page · 6Read PeopleReviewedEN

Arthur Guinness

Irish brewer, entrepreneur and philanthropist (1725–1803) who signed the 9,000-year lease at St James's Gate in Dublin in 1759, scaled a small Leixlip brewery into Ireland's largest beer business and made porter and Guinness stout central to Dublin's brewing identity.

On this page · 6Read PeopleReviewedEN

Aubert de Villaine

French winemaker and co-director of Domaine de la Romanée-Conti (born 1939); returned to Burgundy in 1965, managed DRC with the Leroy family from 1974 to 2021, championed organic and biodynamic farming, founded Domaine de Villaine in Bouzeron with his wife Pamela and led the campaign that made the Climats of Burgundy a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2015.

Audrey Saunders

American bartender and bar owner with no reliable public source for her birth year; she opened Pegu Club in SoHo in 2005 and made fresh juice, measured recipes and repeated training the working standard of the craft cocktail, with the Gin-Gin Mule and Old Cuban as her signature drinks.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Bai Juyi

Tang poet, 772–846; he called himself the Drunken Chanting Gentleman and wrote everyday wine poems about inviting a friend, saying farewell and retiring to the hills.

On this page · 10Read PeopleReviewedEN

Baron Philippe de Rothschild

French estate owner, poet and entrepreneur (1902–1988) who took over Château Mouton Rothschild in 1922, pioneered château bottling, artist labels, Mouton Cadet and the 1973 promotion to First Growth, and co-founded Opus One with Robert Mondavi in 1979.

On this page · 6Read PeopleReviewedEN

Booker Noe

Kentucky bourbon distiller (1929–2004) and sixth-generation master distiller of the Jim Beam family; he took over the Boston, Kentucky plant in 1960, filled the millionth barrel in 1965, launched Booker's in 1987/1988 and built the Small Batch Bourbon Collection in 1992.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Bruno Giacosa

Italian Piedmont winemaker (1929–2018), based in Neive, who began labelling a single Barbaresco vineyard in 1964 and held to long traditional ageing and strict vintage selection, earning the name "the genius of Neive".

Dale DeGroff

An American bartender born in 1948; from 1987 he ran the bar at the Rainbow Room in New York, using fresh juice, properly handled ice and restored classic recipes to drive the cocktail revival, and became known as King Cocktail.

On this page · 9Read PeopleReviewedEN

David Wondrich

An American cocktail historian and writer, born in 1961; author of Imbibe! and Punch and editor-in-chief of The Oxford Companion to Spirits and Cocktails, he used archival research to rewrite the origins of the cocktail.

On this page · 9Read PeopleReviewedEN

Dom Pérignon

A French Benedictine monk, 1638 to 1714, who long managed the cellars of the abbey of Hautvillers in Champagne. Later ages called him the father of champagne, but the claim that he invented it is largely a nineteenth-century commercial construction; his more defensible influence lies in blending, clarification and cellar management.

On this page · 9Read PeopleReviewedEN

Du Fu

Tang poet, 712–770; his drinking poems rarely celebrate excess, dwelling instead on hosting guests, giving up wine in illness and the instability of a wandering life.

On this page · 9Read PeopleReviewedEN

Du Mu

Late Tang poet and prose writer, 803–852; he turns wineshops, wine banners and Apricot Blossom Village into landmarks of travel and city nights, while the widely anthologised "Qingming" is absent from his collected works.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Émile Peynaud

A Bordeaux oenologist, 1912 to 2004, born in Madiran and died in Talence. As professor of oenology at the University of Bordeaux he promoted temperature-controlled fermentation, managed malolactic fermentation, cellar hygiene and systematic tasting, and is regarded as a founder of modern oenology.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Feng Zikai

A modern Chinese painter, essayist and translator, 1898 to 1975, from Chongde in Zhejiang and a student of Master Hongyi. Essays such as The Fine Wine of Shaping, Drinking and A Night Drink by the Lake turn warmed wine, small dishes and late conversation into evidence of ordinary life.

On this page · 10Read PeopleReviewedEN

Frank Schoonmaker

An American wine writer and critic active in the mid-twentieth century, known for urging American wines to be labelled by grape variety and place rather than by borrowed European place names; no reliable public source gives his birth or death year, so both are left empty here.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Fritz Maytag

American brewer and Anchor Brewing owner (born 1937); in 1965 he bought the failing Steam Beer brewery in San Francisco, rebuilt Anchor Steam with open fermentation, whole-cone hops and small-batch production, and became a founding figure of the post-Prohibition American craft-beer movement.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Gary Regan

A British-born American bartender and author, 1951–2019; in The Joy of Mixology he grouped cocktails into families, replacing scattered recipes with a teachable structure, and he created Regan's Orange Bitters No. 6.

On this page · 10Read PeopleReviewedEN

George Garvin Brown

An American whisky entrepreneur, 1846 to 1917; in 1870 in Louisville he founded J.T.S. Brown & Brother and sold Old Forester bourbon in sealed glass bottles, and in 1890 a partnership with the accountant George Forman produced the name Brown-Forman.

George Smith

Distiller in the Livet valley of Speyside (died 1871) who obtained an Elgin licence in 1824 and became the first legal distiller in the Glenlivet parish; he built the plant amid threats from illicit distillers and laid the foundation for The Glenlivet single malt.

Giacomo Conterno

Traditionalist Barolo producer in Monforte d'Alba, Piedmont, who founded the estate that bears his name and established Monfortino through long maceration and large Slavonian oak, making Barolo built for long ageing.

Harry Craddock

An Anglo-American bartender, 1876-1963; head bartender at the American Bar of the Savoy Hotel in London from 1925 and author of The Savoy Cocktail Book (1930), which preserved Prohibition-era recipes as a classic text.

Harry Johnson

A nineteenth-century American bartender and author on bar management, usually dated to 1845; in 1882 he self-published the New and Improved Bartender's Manual, a systematic treatment of bar operation, equipment, staffing and standards that moved bartending from performance toward professional management.

On this page · 9Read PeopleReviewedEN

Henri Jayer

Burgundy winemaker, 1922–2006; farming Vosne-Romanée parcels as a sharecropper, he used low yields, full destemming, cool maceration and restrained new oak to make precise Pinot Noir, and turned the abandoned Cros Parantoux into a premier cru.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Hugh Johnson

A British wine writer born in 1939; he published The World Atlas of Wine in 1971 and has produced Hugh Johnson's Pocket Wine Book since 1977, shaping several generations of readers with maps, vintage tables and regional narrative.

Jack Daniel

American distiller (c. 1850–1911) who registered Distillery No. 1 in Lynchburg, Tennessee, in 1866 and standardised the sugar-maple charcoal filtration known as the Lincoln County Process, defining Tennessee whiskey; his brand won gold in St Louis in 1904 and became one of America's most recognisable whiskies.

On this page · 7Read PeopleReviewedEN

James Buchanan

Scotch whisky blender and brand builder, 1849–1935; born in Canada and raised in Ireland, he set up on his own in London in 1884, built Buchanan Blend, later Black & White, and joined the Distillers Company in 1925.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Jancis Robinson

A British wine writer and editor, born in 1950; she passed the Master of Wine examination in 1984, has written a weekly Financial Times column since 1989, edits The Oxford Companion to Wine and has co-authored The World Atlas of Wine with Hugh Johnson since 2001.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Jerry Thomas

An American bartender, commonly dated 1825 to 1885 (some sources give 1830). In 1862 he published How to Mix Drinks in New York, turning word-of-mouth recipes into a book, and was known as the Professor. The Blue Blazer gave bartending a theatrical side, though he did not invent the classic cocktails.

Jim Barrett

American winery owner (1926–2013) who bought and revived Chateau Montelena in Napa Valley with Ernie Hahn in 1972; the estate's 1973 Chardonnay won the white-wine flight of the 1976 Judgment of Paris.

Jim Koch

Founder of Samuel Adams and The Boston Beer Company (born 1949); in 1984 he brewed his great-great-grandfather's lager recipe at home, launched Boston Lager in Boston bars in 1985, and used national distribution and the Brewing the American Dream programme to shape the American craft-beer industry.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Jimmy Russell

Master distiller at Wild Turkey in Kentucky, who joined in 1954 at eighteen, worked from the floor up and became master distiller in the 1960s; he kept the 101 formula through the bourbon downturn and helped shape Rare Breed, Kentucky Spirit and Russell's Reserve.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

John Jameson

Scottish-born Irish merchant and distiller (1740–1823) who opened the Bow Street distillery in Dublin in 1780; during the malt-tax era he experimented with mixing malted and unmalted barley, laying the basis for Jameson's triple-distilled single-pot-still whiskey.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Josef Groll

Bavarian brewer (1813–1887) who was hired by the citizens of Plzeň in 1842 and brewed the first golden, clear Pilsner lager using soft local water, Saaz hops, Moravian barley and bottom-fermenting yeast, establishing the template for the modern pale lager.

On this page · 6Read PeopleReviewedEN

Ken Grossman

Founder of Sierra Nevada Brewing (born 1955), who opened a homebrew shop in Chico in 1976 and built a brewery from scrap dairy equipment in 1980; Cascade hops and Pale Ale helped drive the American craft-beer revolution, and the company remains family-owned.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Lalou Bize-Leroy

Burgundy merchant and estate owner (born 1932); she took over Maison Leroy in 1955, co-managed Domaine de la Romanée-Conti from 1974 to 1992, founded Domaine Leroy in 1988 and converted it to biodynamics, influencing modern Burgundy through extremely low yields, massal selection and strict sorting.

On this page · 8Read PeopleReviewedEN

Lao She

A modern Chinese novelist and playwright, 1899 to 1966, a Beijinger of Manchu descent. His fiction and drama turn teahouses, wine shops and courtyard compounds into slices of history, and Camel Xiangzi and Teahouse contain verifiable drinking scenes; reliable records of his own drinking are limited and should not be exaggerated.

Li Qingzhao

Song poet, born 1084 and usually said to have died around 1155; her wine lyrics run from "so drunk I did not know the way home" to "three cups, two cups of thin wine", cooling as her life moves from Shandong to the south.

Louis Pasteur

A French chemist and microbiologist, 1822 to 1895; he showed that fermentation is caused by microorganisms and proposed a method of heat treatment that kills them, with a lasting influence on wine, beer and food safety.

Lu Xun

A modern Chinese writer, 1881 to 1936, from Shaoxing in Zhejiang. Kong Yiji and Upstairs in the Wine Shop put Shaoxing yellow wine, warming and tavern hierarchy into fiction, while his 1927 lecture explained Wei-Jin drinking through political pressure.

Lu You

Masataka Taketsuru

A pioneer of Japanese whisky, 1894–1979; he studied distilling and blending in Scotland from 1918 to 1920, helped build the Yamazaki distillery, and founded Dai Nippon Kaju, the predecessor of Nikka, in Yoichi, Hokkaido, in 1934 after parting ways with Shinjiro Torii.

Max Schubert

Australian winemaker (1915–1994) who joined Penfolds as a messenger in 1931, became its first chief winemaker in 1948 and created the experimental Grange in 1951; after the board ordered him to stop in 1957, he continued the 1957–1959 vintages in secret, and Grange was officially reinstated for 1960.

Maynard Amerine

American viticulturist and UC Davis professor (1911–1998) who developed the Winkler heat-summation index with Albert Winkler and standardised wine sensory evaluation through trained panels, blind tasting and statistics.

Michael Broadbent

British wine critic, auctioneer and writer (1927–2020) who rebuilt Christie's wine department in 1966 and recorded tens of thousands of wines in red notebooks, author of The Pocket Guide to Wine Tasting and Vintage Wine.

Michael Jackson (beer writer)

British beer and whisky writer (1942–2007) whose 1977 World Guide to Beer turned style, sensory description and cultural context into a shared language, and whose books and TV series helped Belgian beer, British ale and American craft brewing reach a global audience.

Michel Rolland

A Bordeaux wine consultant, 1947–2026; he combined ripeness assessment, fermentation and maceration control, oak and micro-oxygenation into a consulting process, with clients across many regions, and became a focus of the argument over internationalised style.

Miguel A. Torres

Spanish winemaker and family-business president (born 1941) who joined Familia Torres in 1962, made Mas La Plana in 1970, won the 1979 Paris Wine Olympics, expanded to Chile in 1979 and California in 1982, launched Torres & Earth in 2008 and co-founded the International Wineries for Climate Action in 2019.

Ouyang Xiu

Northern Song writer and statesman, 1007–1072; he called himself the Old Drunkard and made wine the vehicle of landscape pleasure, later listing it among the five objects of his retirement; Chuzhou and Yingzhou are the two places where his relationship with wine is deepest.

Peter Lehmann

Barossa Valley winemaker (1930–2013) who founded Masterson Barossa Vineyards in 1979 to buy grapes from growers facing a vine-pull scheme, renamed the company Peter Lehmann Wines in 1982, and shaped modern Barossa through Stonewell Shiraz and long-term grower contracts.

Piero Antinori

Italian wine producer (born 1938), 26th generation of the Marchesi Antinori family; his Tignanello, first released in 1971, blended Sangiovese with Cabernet and used small oak, rewriting the expression of Tuscan red wine.

Qin Hanzhang

A Chinese food-industry and fermentation engineer, 1908 to 2019; he led the Fenjiu technical trial, took part in research on Maotai and taught at Jiangnan University, and is regarded as one of the founders of scientific winemaking in China.

Robert Mondavi

American winemaker and winery owner (1913–2008) who founded Robert Mondavi Winery in Oakville, Napa Valley, in 1966; championed varietal labelling, French oak and wine tourism, and in 1979 co-founded Opus One with Baron Philippe de Rothschild.

On this page · 7Read PeopleReviewedEN

Robert Parker

An American wine critic, born in 1947; in 1978 he founded the independent newsletter The Wine Advocate, and his 100-point scale shaped the global wine market, while the “Parkerization” debate over homogenised taste made him a focus of the industry. He retired in 2019.

Shinjiro Torii

Japanese entrepreneur (1879–1962) who founded Torii Shoten in Osaka in 1899, launched Akadama Port Wine in 1907, built the Yamazaki distillery in 1923, hired Masataka Taketsuru and released Japan's first commercial malt whisky, Shirofuda, in 1929.

On this page · 7Read PeopleReviewedEN

Steven Spurrier

A British wine merchant, educator and writer, 1941–2021; he founded the Académie du Vin in Paris in 1973, organised the 1976 Judgment of Paris with Patricia Gallagher, and later served as a Decanter consultant editor, shaping international perceptions of California wine and the old hierarchy.

Tang Yin

A Ming dynasty painter and poet, 1470 to 1524, from Wuxian in Suzhou. After an examination scandal he gave up official ambition and sold paintings. The Peach Blossom Cottage Song and Drinking Wine Facing the Moon make flowers, wine and reclusion a position on life, and the official history also records him feigning madness through drink.

Tao Yuanming

Eastern Jin poet, 365–427; he described himself as “addicted to wine” and made drinking part of the same choice as leaving office and returning to the fields.

Wang Wei

Tang poet and painter, 701–761; “A Farewell to Yuan Er on His Mission to Anxi” turns one cup of wine into the definitive Chinese image of farewell, later set to music as the “Three Refrains of Yangguan”.

On this page · 10Read PeopleReviewedEN

Wang Zengqi

A contemporary Chinese writer, 1920 to 1997, from Gaoyou in Jiangsu and a student of Shen Congwen. His food essays treat wine as part of the table, valuing dishes, people and place over prices and display; “a cup of cloudy wine past midday” in “The Rain of Kunming” is his most concentrated passage on drinking.

Warren Winiarski

American Napa Valley winemaker (1928–2024) who founded Stag's Leap Wine Cellars in 1973; his 1973 Cabernet Sauvignon won the red-wine flight of the 1976 Judgment of Paris and helped bring California wine international attention.

William Grant

A Scottish whisky entrepreneur, 1839 to 1923; he worked his way up at Mortlach, built Glenfiddich with his family from 1886, ran the first spirit on Christmas Day 1887 and added Balvenie in 1892; the company remains family-owned.

Xin Qiji

Southern Song poet and soldier, 1140–1207; after crossing south from the Jin-occupied north in his twenties, he spent two long retirements writing ci in which wine opens memory, questions old age and mocks its own consolation.

Yu Dafu

A modern Chinese writer, 1896 to 1945, from Fuyang in Zhejiang and a founding member of the Creation Society. His classical-style poems and prose often mention wine, and in 1942 he ran the Zhao Yuji distillery in Sumatra under the alias Zhao Lian; he was killed in 1945.

Zhang Bishi

A Chinese industrialist and overseas merchant, 1841 to 1916; in 1892 he invested in the Changyu wine company in Yantai, Shandong, bringing European grape varieties, equipment and winemakers to China and helping to establish a modern wine industry there.

Zheng Banqiao

A Qing dynasty painter, calligrapher and poet, 1693 to 1766; he served as magistrate of Fan and Wei counties, then sold paintings in Yangzhou as one of the Eight Eccentrics. His inscriptions and his published price list place wine, painting prices and livelihood on the same line.

Zhou Henggang

A Chinese baijiu technologist, 1918 to 2004; he led the 1955 and 1962 Yantai trials and the 1964-1966 Maotai trial, summarised the Yantai method, and advanced research on fuqu, caproic-acid bacteria and pit mud.
