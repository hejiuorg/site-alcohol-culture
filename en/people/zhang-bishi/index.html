Zhang Bishi

A Chinese industrialist and overseas merchant, 1841 to 1916; in 1892 he invested in the Changyu wine company in Yantai, Shandong, bringing European grape varieties, equipment and winemakers to China and helping to establish a modern wine industry there.

Zhang Bishi (1841-1916), whose given name was Zhenxun and whose courtesy name was Bishi, was born in Dabu, Guangdong. He was an overseas Chinese merchant, industrialist and consular official, and the founder of the Changyu wine company. He is often called the "father of Chinese wine", but the label mixes two achievements: he brought European vines, equipment and cellar practice to Yantai, yet he was not a winemaker. His strength lay in raising capital, organising an enterprise and working through official networks.

Life

Zhang was born in Chelunping village, Dabu county. His father was a xiucai who taught and practised medicine. He had only a few years of schooling before leaving for the south seas around 1856, and he first worked as an apprentice in a Chinese-owned paper shop in Batavia, now Jakarta.

Over the next three decades he moved into plantations, shipping, mining, banking and medicine. He ran plantation companies in Batavia, Penang and Singapore, grew coconuts, coffee, tea and rubber, founded steamship companies, invested in tin and established a bank. At his peak his assets were reckoned in the tens of millions of taels, making him one of the richest Chinese merchants in Southeast Asia.

In the 1890s he served as Chinese consul in Penang and then consul-general in Singapore, and around 1904 he became a commissioner for overseas commerce. This double role let him mobilise Southeast Asian capital while dealing directly with Li Hongzhang and Sheng Xuanhuai.

Timeline

  • 1841: born in Dabu, Guangdong.
  • Around 1856: left for Batavia and worked in a paper shop before going into business.
  • 1892: went to Yantai at Sheng Xuanhuai's invitation and invested in Changyu.
  • 1893: Changyu received Qing approval, with a fifteen-year patent and three years of tax exemption in Zhili, Fengtian and Shandong.
  • 1912: Sun Yat-sen visited Changyu and wrote the inscription "Pinzhong Liquan".
  • 1915: Changyu products won awards at the Panama-Pacific International Exposition.
  • 12 September 1916: died in Batavia, aged seventy-five.

Work in the wine trade

In 1892 Zhang surveyed Yantai and concluded that its hills, sea air, soil and climate suited wine grapes. He bought land for vineyards and a factory near the shore, imported more than a hundred varieties from Europe, brought in equipment and hired Chinese and foreign winemakers, with his nephew Zhang Yingdong as general manager.

Three elements mattered. The vineyards were divided by variety, so growing and winemaking were managed inside one company. The underground cellar, oak-barrel ageing, glass bottles and cork closures gave the products the basic form of industrial wine. And in 1893 the company obtained a fifteen-year patent and three years of tax exemption in three provinces.

The range was not limited to red wine; after the 1915 exposition Koya brandy was renamed "Gold Medal Brandy", a name still in use.

Major works and institutions

Zhang's first work was the company itself: Changyu was one of the first Chinese wine companies to organise growing, fermentation, ageing and bottling on an industrial scale, linking cellar, barrels, bottles and corks into one chain rather than selling bulk wine. His second was the product range. His third was a text: in 1906 he wrote "An Account of the Founding of the Changyu Wine Company Limited", describing the purchase of land and machinery, the building of the factory and cellar, and the advance of capital.

The fifteen-year patent and three-year tax exemption were special policies under the late Qing slogan of "saving the country through industry". They protected the enterprise, but they also showed how immature the market and legal environment were.

Influence

First, Changyu turned wine production into a concrete branch of Chinese industry, organising growing, winemaking, ageing, packaging and sales within one modern factory system. Second, it established Yantai as a modern Chinese wine region; Shandong is one of the earliest Chinese regions recognised internationally on the wine map. Third, it provided a model of overseas Chinese capital returning home. Fourth, it left a brand: after 1915, Gold Medal Brandy became one of the few early Chinese drinks with an international reputation.

Assessment and disputes

The most often quoted assessment is Sun Yat-sen's 1912 inscription "Pinzhong Liquan", which links the quality of the wine to the classical image of a sweet spring. Local histories and mainstream media often call Zhang the "father of Chinese wine" or a "patriotic overseas Chinese and national industrialist".

There are three disputes. First, his birth year: the Yantai Institute of Local History gives 1840, while the Institute of Modern History of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences and People's Daily Online give 1841. This page uses 1841 and writes "around 1856" for his departure. Second, the founding year: Changyu is usually dated to 1892, but Qing approval came in 1893, and some studies treat 1894 or 1895 as the year of formal establishment or first production. Third, the source of his wealth: local histories state that in Southeast Asia he contracted for liquor tax, pawnshop tax and, in some areas, opium tax.

The claim that Changyu was "China's first wine company" also needs qualification. It is usually regarded as China's first large-scale, industrial modern wine enterprise, but winemaking in China long predates 1892.

Common misreadings

First, that Zhang was a winemaker. He was not; he raised money, chose the site, managed official relations and organised the venture, while Chinese and foreign technicians did the winemaking.

Second, that Changyu was already built and producing in 1892. 1892 was the year of investment and founding; approval came in 1893, and the vineyards, cellar and factory were built over the following years.

Third, that the 1915 gold medal meant "the best in the world". The exposition offered several classes of award, and several Changyu products were honoured.

Fourth, that Changyu was the earliest wine in China. Grapes and wine were recorded in China as early as the Han dynasty; Changyu's significance lies in modern industrial organisation.

Fifth, that Zhang worked only in wine. He also operated plantations, shipping, mining, banking and medicine, and he donated to schools.
