Qin Hanzhang

A Chinese food-industry and fermentation engineer, 1908 to 2019; he led the Fenjiu technical trial, took part in research on Maotai and taught at Jiangnan University, and is regarded as one of the founders of scientific winemaking in China.

Qin Hanzhang (1908-2019) was a food-industry and fermentation engineer from Wuxi, Jiangsu. Trained in Europe in fermentation and agricultural processing, he brought analytical chemistry, microbiology and factory trials into the production of baijiu, beer, wine and soy sauce. He led the Fenjiu technical trial, took part in research on Maotai, and taught at Jiangnan University.

Life

Qin was born in February 1908 in Zhangshe township, Wuxi county, Jiangsu. He studied at the agriculture college of National Labour University in Shanghai, graduating in 1931. He went to Belgium on a Boxer Indemnity scholarship, graduated from the State Agricultural Institute of Gembloux in 1935, and studied brewing at the fermentation institute of Berlin University in 1936.

After returning to China in September 1936, he taught at Fudan University, Southwest Associated University, National Central University and the private Jiangnan University. In 1949 he became head of the agricultural-products manufacturing department at Jiangnan University. In 1950 he was appointed counsellor to the Ministry of Food Industry. He became director of the fermentation research institute of the First Ministry of Light Industry in 1960 and of the food fermentation research institute in 1978.

He remained active in writing and industry affairs and died in Beijing on 15 August 2019.

Work in the drinks trade

Qin's central contribution was to turn experience into a measurable process. From February 1964 to May 1965, at Zhou Enlai's request, he led the "Fenjiu trial". The trial broke Fenjiu production into raw materials, starter culture, fermentation, distillation and storage, measured acids, esters and alcohols, and adjusted the workshop process. In 1978 it received a major achievement award at the National Science Conference.

From 1975 he led chemical analyses of Maotai for eight years and concluded that the town's particular natural environment and climate meant Maotai could not be produced elsewhere. The conclusion was often cited as the scientific basis for that saying.

He worked on bottle haze at the Qingdao brewery and helped companies including Great Wall, Changyu and Shaoxing wine solve technical problems, his work spanning baijiu, beer, wine and yellow rice wine.

Major works and institutions

Qin's main contribution was to write winemaking knowledge into teachable texts. His books include "The Technology and Experimental Methods of Chinese Baijiu", "Theory and Technology of Soy Sauce Brewing", "Analytical Chemistry of Wine", "Technology of Brewing Famous Quality Baijiu" and "New Wine Classic". According to The Beijing News, he wrote, co-wrote or edited more than 40 books totalling nearly 60 million characters.

At the institutional level he helped build the disciplines of food and fermentation in China. He headed the agricultural-products manufacturing department at Jiangnan University in 1949, helped found 15 light-industry schools including the Wuxi Institute of Light Industry around 1958, and used the research institute to spread trial results to factories in the 1960s.

Scientific method

Qin's method bore clear marks of his European training: establish analytical indicators, run comparative trials, then write the results back into the process. He valued laboratory analysis but did not treat it as an end in itself; acids, esters and alcohols had to explain temperature, humidity, starter culture and fermentation cycles in the workshop. He also emphasised the "trial" as an organisation: experts, factories and local technicians gathered in one place for a limited time to solve a specific problem.

Influence

First, he moved the technical language of baijiu closer to modern food science. Practices once passed down by masters, such as judging the cut by the shape of the distillate or grading by aroma, acquired explanations in terms of acidity, esters, microbes and temperature.

Second, he trained a generation of industry specialists. Graduates of Jiangnan University, the Wuxi Institute of Light Industry and the food fermentation institute later became technical leaders at Maotai, Fenjiu, Yanghe and Red Star.

Third, he helped make the "trial" a routine way of solving common problems in the industry: concentrate expertise, summarise the process, then spread it nationwide.

Assessment and disputes

Jiangnan University's obituary called Qin "a doyen of China's wine industry" and "a pioneer and academic leader of food science and industrial fermentation and brewing in New China". Ji Keliang, former chairman of Maotai, said at the farewell ceremony: "I admired him greatly; he was a respected teacher with clean hands."

There are two disputes. The first is the attribution of the Maotai trial. Jiangnan University's biography says that in 1964 the trial was led and joined by Fang Xinfang and Qin Hanzhang, while other industry accounts credit Zhou Henggang with its organisation. The safer statement is that it was a multi-person, multi-institution project, and Qin was responsible for part of the chemical analysis and technical summary. The second is the conclusion that Maotai cannot be produced elsewhere. It came from analyses begun in 1975 and lasting eight years; supporters treat it as origin science, while critics say it turns complex ecological and craft differences into a single conclusion.

Common misreadings

First, that Qin founded Jiangnan University. He did not; in 1949 he was appointed head of its agricultural-products manufacturing department.

Second, that Qin invented Fenjiu or Maotai. He invented no traditional famous liquor; he summarised processes, ran analyses and spread techniques.

Third, that Qin studied only baijiu. He also worked on beer, wine, yellow rice wine, soy sauce and the baking industry, and helped build light-industry schools.

Fourth, that all 60 million characters were written by him alone. The figure from The Beijing News includes books he wrote, co-wrote and edited, many of them textbooks, reviews and compilations.

Fifth, that the Fenjiu and Maotai trials were the same event. They differed in time, place, aims and participants; Qin led the Fenjiu trial, while the Maotai trial was a collaboration often associated with Zhou Henggang.
