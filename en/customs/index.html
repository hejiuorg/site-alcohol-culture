New Year Drinking: Pepper-and-Cypress Wine and Tusu Wine

Drinking pepper-and-cypress wine and tusu wine on the first day of the first lunar month is a seasonal custom recorded at least as early as the Southern Dynasties; the Jingchu Suishi Ji also notes that “the order of drinking begins with the youngest”. Wine here combines plague protection, New Year blessing and family order, not merely festive cheer.

Cold Food Festival and wine

The Cold Food Festival falls a day or two before Qingming and centres on a fire ban, cold food and tomb visits; wine plays the role of a libation and a family-meal drink rather than a fixed seasonal recipe. This entry covers timing, origins, vessels, food, modern practice and health limits.

Dragon Boat realgar wine

Drinking realgar wine, sprinkling realgar water and painting a character on children’s foreheads are documented Han festival customs. Realgar contains arsenic and heating produces an even more toxic compound; this entry separates origins and symbolism from the safety boundary and offers safer substitutes.

Harvest wine and grape-harvest customs

Harvest wine is the drink of thanksgiving, new-taste tasting and village meals after the autumn harvest; in European vineyard regions the same impulse continues as the grape harvest festival. This entry compares Chinese and European forms of harvest drinking, their timing, vessels, food and modern tourist packaging.

Housewarming wine

Housewarming wine is the meal and drink offered after a new home is finished or a family moves in; in the north it is often called “warming the pot” or “searing the pot bottom”, in the south “entering the household”. It confirms the joy of settling in, the neighbourhood relationship and social obligations, and raises real questions about pressure to drink, display and indoor pollution.

Wedding Wine Customs: From Hejin to the Cross-Cup Drink

Hejin was a shared-drinking rite in the pre-Qin wedding ceremony. The Liji says “hejin er yin”, and the Yili records “the third ceremonial drink uses a jin”. Later practice developed into the cross-cup drink, but the ancient and modern rites, vessels and meanings are not identical.

Zhongyuan Festival and wine

The Zhongyuan Festival on the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month is observed as a Taoist festival and as the Buddhist Ullambana; its core is ancestor remembrance, paper offerings and river lanterns. Wine serves mainly as a libation and a family-meal drink, turning grief into a gesture.
