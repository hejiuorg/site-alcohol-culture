The International Organisation of Vine and Wine: from the 1924 wine office to today

The OIV was founded in Paris in 1924 as the Office International du Vin, driven by international cooperation after the phylloxera crisis and by the need to govern fraudulent wine trade. Reorganised in 2001 under its present name, it is based in Dijon, France; it sets harmonised standards, statistics and research, but its standards do not automatically become member-state law.

The Scotch Whisky Association: how an industry guards a name

The Scotch Whisky Association (SWA) traces its history to 1912 and is based in Edinburgh. It is the trade association of the Scotch whisky industry, representing more than 90 member companies that account for 97% of Scotch whisky by production. It defends the legal protection and technical definition of Scotch Whisky and works on tax, trade and sustainability; the current legal entity is a company limited by guarantee registered in 1960, and the association is not a regulator.

Brewers Association: the trade body that defines small and independent

The Brewers Association (BA) grew out of the American Homebrewers Association founded in 1978 and earlier small-brewer bodies, and took its present form in 2005 when the Association of Brewers merged with the Brewers' Association of America. It represents small and independent US breweries, maintains the craft brewer definition, runs the Great American Beer Festival and advocates in Washington. It is not a government regulator.

China Alcohol Circulation Association: from commerce to circulation

The China Alcohol Circulation Association was formally established in April 1995 as the China Liquor Commerce Association and renamed in April 2006 with the approval of the Ministry of Civil Affairs, which recognises it as a national first-tier association. It is based in Beijing and serves liquor producers and distributors through self-discipline, market information, training and manufacturer-retailer coordination. Its national assured-liquor demonstration store programme began in 2014 and had recognised nearly 2,000 stores by 2025, covering 31 provincial-level regions.

China Alcoholic Drinks Association: from an industrial body to a cross-category trade organisation

The China Alcoholic Drinks Association (CADA) was originally the China Brewing Industry Association. It was approved by the former Ministry of Light Industry and registered with the Ministry of Civil Affairs on 22 June 1992. Its registration authority is the Ministry of Civil Affairs and its business guidance comes from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology. It covers baijiu, beer, wine and yellow wine, runs group standards, exhibitions and industry data, but it is not an administrative agency and does not issue production licences.

CNFIA: a food-industry body that also trains baijiu judges

The China National Food Industry Association (CNFIA) was approved by the State Council and founded on 29 October 1981 as the self-regulatory industry body of China's food industry, with the stated functions of coordination, planning, liaison, guidance and service. It has a baijiu professional committee and from 1984 has organised the national baijiu judge corps; it is not a regulator, and food safety standards and market enforcement belong to government.

Comité Champagne: How Growers and Houses Manage a Region Together

The Comité Champagne, formerly the CIVC, was founded in 1941 as the interprofessional body of Champagne growers and houses. It coordinates the region's economic, technical, environmental and promotional work and protects the Champagne designation abroad; the rules of the AOC are still set and recognised by INAO, the French national institute for origin and quality.

Court of Master Sommeliers: turning restaurant wine service into an exam

The Court of Master Sommeliers (CMS) is not a medieval order but an examining body that grew out of the needs of British restaurant wine service. The first Master Sommelier examination was held in London in 1969, and the Court was formally established in 1977. Its four levels turn a sommelier's experience into examinable knowledge and service, but the title is neither a licence to practise nor a government certification.

DISCUS: The US Spirits Trade's Voice in Washington

The Distilled Spirits Council of the United States (DISCUS) was formed in 1973 by the merger of the Bourbon Institute, the Distilled Spirits Institute and Licensed Beverage Industries. It is a national trade association headquartered at 101 Constitution Avenue NW in Washington, D.C. It lobbies at federal, state and local level, runs a voluntary advertising code and publishes industry data; it is not a regulator, since licensing, tax and label approval remain with government.

FEV: gathering the voice of Spanish wineries

The Federación Española del Vino (FEV), founded in 1978, is Spain's national private non-profit winery association, representing more than 950 wineries and 17 associations whose combined output is about 83% of the value of Spanish bottled wine. It works on policy, industry coordination, market observation and sustainability certification, and is not a regulator; its statutes are deposited with the Spanish Ministry of Labour and Social Economy, with the latest amendment filed on 18 November 2020.

Institute of Masters of Wine: a professional community born from an exam

The Institute of Masters of Wine (IMW) began with an examination in 1953. Twenty-one candidates sat it and six passed; those six founded the Institute in 1955. Based in London but international in membership, the IMW examines theory, blind tasting and an individual research paper, and requires members to sign a code of conduct. It is not a regulator and does not grant a licence to practise.

Japan Sake and Shochu Makers Association: The Trade Body for Sake, Shochu and Mirin

The Japan Sake and Shochu Makers Association (JSS) was founded in December 1953 under the Act on Securing of Liquor Tax and on Liquor Business Associations. It is the national trade body for producers of sake, honkaku shochu, awamori and hon mirin, with about 1,600 member companies. It promotes, researches and coordinates, but it is not an enforcement agency: licensing and tax matters belong to government.

Korea Alcohol Industry Association: The Trade Body for Soju and Other Drinks

The Korea Alcohol Industry Association was founded in November 1980 and represents companies making soju, cheongju, whisky and other drinks. It promotes the industry, carries out research and informs the public, but it is not a regulator: licensing, tax and distribution rules belong to government, and its statistics cannot replace official statistics.

New Zealand Winegrowers: Growers and Wineries in One Organisation

New Zealand Winegrowers (NZW), established in March 2002, is the national organisation for the country's grape and wine sector, with more than 600 grower members and 700 winery members. It represents both growing and winemaking, and works on promotion, research, industry data and SWNZ sustainability certification. It is not a government body; export and food-safety rules belong to government.

Tequila Regulatory Council: Who Certifies That a Bottle of Tequila Is Genuine

The Tequila Regulatory Council (CRT) was founded in Mexico on 17 May 1994 as a civil, non-profit organisation based in Zapopan, Jalisco. It brings agave growers, tequila producers, bottlers, distributors and government agencies into one body, inspects and certifies compliance with NOM-006-SCFI-2012, and defends the Tequila appellation in Mexico and abroad. It is accredited and approved by Mexican bodies, but it is not a government department.

The Anti-Saloon League: the pressure group that wrote prohibition into the Constitution

The Anti-Saloon League began in 1893 as a state-level body in Ohio and became a national organisation in 1895, drawing mainly on evangelical Protestant churches to press legislators at every level of government. After the Eighteenth Amendment it pushed for strict enforcement; once prohibition was repealed in 1933 it lost its political weight, merging into the National Temperance League in 1950.

The Bordeaux wine trade council: growers and merchants at one table

The CIVB, created by law on 18 August 1948, is the interprofessional body of Bordeaux winegrowers and merchants. It was recognised as an interprofessional organisation in 2014 and reorganised by decree in 2016. It works on promotion, market research and technical and economic matters, but does not enforce AOC rules, which fall to France's INAO.

The European Committee of Wine Enterprises (CEEV)

The Comité Européen des Entreprises Vins (CEEV) is a Brussels-based European wine trade body representing wine companies and national associations in EU discussions on wine policy, labelling, promotion and trade.

ViniPortugal: an interprofessional promoter of Portuguese wine

ViniPortugal was founded on 15 October 1996 as a private, non-profit interprofessional body whose members are nine associations for trade, production, cooperatives, distillation, agriculture and protected appellations. It promotes Portuguese wine, runs the Wines of Portugal brand created in 2010, and in 2015 was recognised by the Ministry of Agriculture as the wine interprofessional organisation.

Vinos de Chile: presenting Chilean wine as a whole abroad

Vinos de Chile, also known as Wines of Chile, was formed in 2007 when Viñas de Chile and ChileVid merged. It is a private non-profit trade association based in Vitacura, Santiago, working on export promotion, market intelligence and industry coordination, and running a sustainability certification through its technology consortium. It is not a regulator.

Wine Australia: A Statutory Body for Research, Promotion and Export Control

Wine Australia was established on 1 July 2014 under the Wine Australia Act 2013. It is a Commonwealth Government statutory authority whose functions cover research and innovation, market promotion, and the regulation of wine exports. It is funded by statutory industry levies and matched government research funding, and is bound by a statutory funding agreement that bars it from industry political advocacy.

Wine Institute: public policy advocacy for California wineries

Wine Institute, formed in 1934 by the merger of two predecessor bodies, is the public policy advocacy association of the California wine industry. It represents about 1,000 wineries and affiliated businesses, is headquartered in Sacramento, works on policy at state, federal and international level, runs export promotion and in 2003 co-created the California Sustainable Winegrowing Alliance with the California Association of Winegrape Growers. It is not an enforcement body.

Wines of South Africa: Taking South African Wine to Export Markets

Wines of South Africa (WoSA) is a non-profit organisation owned by the South African wine industry and funded by levies, dedicated to promoting South African wine in international markets. Its head office is in Stellenbosch, with offices in London, New York and Hong Kong and agencies in Canada, Sweden, the Netherlands and Germany. It does not sell wine and does not certify origin; public material does not record a founding year.

WSET: turning tasting into an examinable qualification

WSET (Wine & Spirit Education Trust), founded in London in 1969, is a British wine and spirits education body that is both an awarding body and a registered charity, with qualifications recognised by Ofqual. It offers graded courses from beginner to the Level 4 Diploma, turning tasting, regions, production and regulation into examinable content, but a certificate is not a licence to practise.
